## Step 1 — The Template (verbatim from the library)

The [Shift Handoff prompt](../../prompts/daily-operations.md) as it ships:

```text
Turn these non-sensitive notes into a shift handoff for the incoming FedEx FEC supervisor or manager.

Outgoing shift: [Day/Evening/Night]
Incoming shift: [Day/Evening/Night]
Tone: direct, calm, and action-oriented

Notes (scrubbed):
[Paste notes. Remove all real names, addresses, tracking numbers, and package details.]

Return:
1. What changed since the last handoff
2. What is still open
3. High-risk items (safety, customer, linehaul)
4. Who owns each next step (roles, not names)
5. What to check first in the next shift
6. One safety reminder
```

Notice the safety rule is *inside* the prompt — "scrubbed," "roles, not names."
You can't use the prompt correctly and leak data at the same time.

## Step 2 — Filled In (synthetic scenario)

Messy notes, the way they actually get typed at 17:55:

```text
Outgoing shift: Day
Incoming shift: Evening

Notes (scrubbed):
belt 2 jammed twice, maintenance looked at it, says bearing, part ordered eta thur.
2 feeder trucks late from ramp, second one still not here (due 1630).
short 3 handlers tonight per schedule. one call-out.
customer escalation from this morning resolved, no follow-up needed.
ice expected on dock ramp after 2000 per weather, salt bin is low.
```

## Step 3 — The Real Output

The following was generated by an AI model (Claude, Anthropic) on 2026-07-16
from exactly the filled prompt above — unedited:

```text
SHIFT HANDOFF — Day → Evening

1. What changed since the last handoff
- Belt 2 jammed twice; maintenance diagnosed a failing bearing. Part ordered, ETA Thursday.
- Two feeder trucks ran late from the ramp; one has arrived, the second (due 16:30) is still outstanding.
- This morning's customer escalation is resolved — no follow-up needed.

2. What is still open
- Second feeder truck not yet arrived.
- Belt 2 running but unrepaired until the part lands Thursday.
- Salt bin low ahead of expected dock-ramp ice.

3. High-risk items
- SAFETY: Ice expected on the dock ramp after 20:00 with a low salt bin.
- LINEHAUL: Outstanding feeder truck may compress the evening sort window.
- EQUIPMENT: Belt 2 could jam again under load before Thursday's repair.

4. Who owns each next step
- Evening supervisor: confirm feeder truck status with ramp dispatch on arrival.
- Dock lead: salt the ramp before 20:00; flag if the bin runs out.
- Maintenance liaison: confirm Thursday part ETA and schedule the belt 2 repair.
- Evening supervisor: rebalance staffing for the 3-handler shortage (1 call-out).

5. What to check first
- Has the second feeder truck arrived? If not, get an ETA from dispatch immediately.

6. Safety reminder
- Treat the dock ramp as a slip hazard after 20:00 — salt early, post a cone if
  conditions turn, and keep three points of contact on vehicle steps.
```

## Step 4 — The Part the AI Can't Do

The draft above is good — and it still needs a human. Only the outgoing
supervisor knows whether "resolved" really means resolved, whether the bearing
diagnosis is trusted, and whether three handlers short is survivable tonight.
The AI saved the ten minutes of formatting and recall; the judgment call stays
with the person whose name is on the shift.

That division — **AI drafts, human owns** — is the entire operating model of
this hub, from this humble prompt all the way up to the
[deployed dashboard](logistics-intelligence-system.md) and the
[ADK agent](adk-shift-brief-agent.md).
