Statistical Process Control (what's this?)

The statistically defensible layer. Control limits come from the data's own week-to-week variation (σ̂ = MR̄/1.128). Points or patterns breaking the rules are flagged as special-cause — worth investigating, not just noise.

👀 What you're looking at: the green middle line is the facility's average. The dashed red lines are the edges of "normal." An orange dot is a week (or run of weeks) the math says is genuinely unusual.
✅ What to do: if the top cards say "In control," the facility is behaving — don't chase noise. If "Special cause" or "Trending," read the flagged weeks below and go find the cause.

Forecast

An ensemble of simple, robust models (SES + damped-Holt + linear trend), auto-weighted by walk-forward accuracy. The widening band is the message — near-term is useful, far-term is directional.

👀 What you're looking at: the dashed orange line is the best-guess next few weeks. The shaded band is "we're fairly sure it lands in here." It gets wider further out because further out is less certain.
✅ What to do: trust next week or two for planning; treat 6+ weeks as a direction, not a promise. Read the band, not just the line.

Backtest — walk-forward accuracy (lower MASE is better; <1 beats naïve)

How to read MASE: it compares each model to a naïve "next week = this week" guess. MASE < 1 means the model beats that baseline. We auto-select the lowest-MASE model but prefer the simpler one when the difference is under 5% — on ~25 points, chasing tiny accuracy gains usually means fitting noise.

Method & Caveats

Why this is defensible to leadership — and where it is not.

One-line thesis: Traders and quality engineers independently invented the same toolkit (moving averages, bands, momentum, crossovers). On ~25 weekly operations points, the SPC version of each tool carries a quantified false-alarm rate and is the one to alert on. The TA version is the same shape with friendlier labels — excellent for communicating to managers, but it should not drive automated decisions.

What each tab is doing

  • Trend & Signals — SMA(4) month baseline + SMA(13) quarter baseline, a Bollinger(8, 2σ) envelope, and 4/13 crossover detection (the "golden/death cross" analog). Lagging confirmation of regime, not prediction.
  • Process Control — the rigorous engine. I-MR individuals chart with 3σ limits from σ̂=MR̄/1.128; Nelson rules 1/3/5/6 (outlier, trend, emerging shift); EWMA(λ=0.2) for slow drift; tabular CUSUM (k=0.5σ, h=5σ) for earliest sustained-shift alarm.
  • Momentum (TA) — ROC (legible MoM/QoQ change), and MACD/RSI labeled indicative only because at this sample size their overbought/oversold "reversal" calls have no mechanism in ops data.
  • Forecast — SES, damped-Holt (φ=0.9), and OLS linear trend, simple-averaged, with a trend gate from EMA-crossover + ROC. 80% bands from residuals, widening with horizon. Walk-forward (rolling-origin) MASE selection with a simplicity prior.

The honest caveats

  • Small sample (~25 weeks). Control limits are provisional until ~25+ points and should be re-baselined as data accrues. RSI(14)/MACD(26)/50-200 crosses are impossible here — all periods are deliberately shortened.
  • Autocorrelation. This week resembles last week (same crew, lanes, mix). That inflates false "trend" signals and narrows bands optimistically. Lag-1 autocorrelation is shown on the SPC tab; treat single-indicator blips skeptically.
  • The goal is engineered & re-baselined. A change in the goal denominator or a peak-season shift looks like a process shift. Segment by goal regime before trusting a long-run signal.
  • Ops KPIs are not tradeable prices. There is no momentum trade and no mean-reversion mechanism. A facility at "RSI 75" is not "due for a pullback."
  • Multiple comparisons. Running many rules × many facilities × weekly guarantees spurious signals. Require corroboration across ≥2 indicators before escalating.

Recommended use

Alert on SPC (I-MR + Nelson 1/3/5/6, then EWMA/CUSUM for drift). Use TA overlays to explain the picture to managers. Use the forecast for near-term planning (1–3 weeks confidently), reading the band, not the line. Always pair a flagged facility with the relevant operational playbook (lanefull / rehandle best practices) before acting.

Plain-language glossary

Signal — a nudge to go investigate, not a verdict. The tool surfaces them; people decide.
In control / Special cause — "in control" = normal random wiggle. "Special cause" = something specific happened that week (outage, weather, staffing) worth finding.
Trending — the average itself is climbing or sliding week over week, not just bouncing. The control limits intentionally step aside here.
σ̂ (sigma-hat) / control limits — the normal week-to-week spread, and the dashed lines at 3× that spread marking the edge of "normal."
SPC — Statistical Process Control: the factory quality-control method behind the control charts and rules.
SMA / smoothing — a rolling average that ignores one-week blips so the real direction shows.
Rate of Change (ROC) — how much higher or lower than a month (or quarter) ago, in percent.
MACD / RSI — "stock-chart" momentum indicators. Useful as visuals; indicative only on weekly ops data — don't act off them alone.
Forecast band — the shaded "we're fairly sure it lands in here" range. Wider = less certain. Read the band, not just the line.
MASE — a forecast accuracy score. Below 1 means it beats a naïve "next week = this week" guess.
$/point — the recap's conversion: each efficiency point ≈ $50M/year network-wide, so weekly $ = gap × $50M ÷ 52.
Lanefull / secondary LF / rehandle — execution problems on the dock; "secondary LF is 100% controllable" per the playbook.
