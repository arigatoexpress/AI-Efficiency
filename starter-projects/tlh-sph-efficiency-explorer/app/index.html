Method & caveats

The identity

Volume = TLH × SPH, so SPH = Volume / TLH. Efficiency goals ride on SPH vs an engineered goal, so the question "did efficiency really improve?" is the question "which side of the division moved?"

The exact decomposition

With last week (0) and this week (1):

  • SPH effect (real throughput, hours held at base): (vol1 − vol0) / tlh0
  • TLH effect (hours lever, evaluated at current volume): vol1/tlh1 − vol1/tlh0

These two sum exactly to sph1 − sph0 — no residual (a Laspeyres-style split: the throughput effect is weighted at base hours, the hours effect at current volume). The app verifies |(sphE + tlhE) − ΔSPH| < 1e-9 for every facility on every render and would flag a failure in the status line.

Validation cases (run at startup)

  • Pure SPH: hours flat, volume up → ~100% of the change is the SPH effect, TLH effect ≈ 0.
  • Pure TLH: volume held, hours cut (SPH rises mechanically) → SPH effect ≈ 0, ~100% TLH effect.
  • Proportional: volume and hours up by the same factor (SPH flat) → the two effects exactly offset; total ≈ 0.

Note: a naive split that uses the raw SPH delta as the "SPH effect" assigns half of a pure hours-only move to throughput — that is wrong, and it is why this tool uses the formulas above.

Driver tag

With aS = |SPH effect|, aT = |TLH effect|: SPH-driven if aS/(aS+aT) ≥ 0.65, TLH-driven if aT/(aS+aT) ≥ 0.65, else Mixed.

The dollar figure

Weekly $ = effect × ($ per SPH-point per year) ÷ 52. It is a planning translation for sizing attention, not accounting — do not present it as realized savings.

Caveats

  • Two weeks is a noisy window. A single week-over-week split tells you where to look, not whether a trend is real. Pair it with the Dock Efficiency Signal Lab's SPC charts before escalating.
  • An hours-driven "gain" needs verification. Confirm service, safety, and volume held before communicating it as a win — that is the single biggest misread this tool exists to prevent.
  • Volume drops depress SPH even when the operation ran well (fixed sort minimums); a negative SPH effect can be a demand story, not a performance story.
  • Aggregate data only. Facility/shift totals — never individual employee hours or performance.
